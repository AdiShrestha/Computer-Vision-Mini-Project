## The sign-flip disagreement is not necessarily negligible

Claude reports 297/300 matches to a float-enumeration reference and calls the residual negligible. Its inputs/script were not supplied, and an ordinary floating summation is not automatically the authoritative exact reference. I tested exact rational sums of the stored binary64 differences. For `[1, 2^-55, 2^-56, 2^-57, 2^-58]`, the former rounded-mean comparisons return **0.625**, whereas exact sign enumeration returns **0.0625**. At this sample size the minimum two-sided p-value is 2/32. Rounding merged genuinely distinct signed sums into ties; this is materially larger than a negligible rounding difference.

The repair converts each finite difference to its exact integer/power-of-two representation and uses a common denominator. Compare the absolute integer sums; the mean's common `n` divisor cancels. Exact and Monte Carlo paths use that statistic, without an arbitrary numerical tolerance. Monte Carlo retains add-one correction. Regression cases use an independent `Fraction` enumeration with cancellation, tiny values, zeros and positive contrasts. A 17-unit Monte Carlo case checks the plus-one floor.

These computations refer to stored finite binary64 differences after subtraction. They do not recover infinitely precise physical contrasts, validate exchangeability, turn seeds into independent events, or make a small event corpus sufficient. Effect/CI fields retain their stated floating estimators and conditional interpretation. The inference assumptions in the plan remain necessary.
