\begin{abstract}This study develops a source-traceable Sentinel-2 time-series pipeline for South Lhonak and Khangchung Tsho, using 141 retained acquisitions from 2018--2023. A missingness-aware temporal autoencoder was trained on observations assigned to FIT and selected using a separate VAL period. After correcting a causal-attention failure under leading missing observations, a frozen re-execution completed 50 fits across primary, capacity and factorial configurations. An independent validation replay matched all selected checkpoints across 350 target-bearing VAL blocks; 80 blocks without valid targets were recorded as unavailable. For the five-seed primary configuration, mean selected VAL masked MSE was 0.8498 (SD 0.2371). These are optimization diagnostics on one two-lake corpus, not independent site-level replications. The corrected models have not been evaluated on EVAL, and event detection, false-alarm rates, seasonal generalization and operational warning performance are not established. The study is exploratory and limited by two sites, unvalidated candidate zones and incomplete event/control evidence.\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} glacial lakes; Sentinel-2; anomaly detection; missing data; temporal validation; exploratory analysis
\section*{1. Introduction}
Glacial-lake monitoring is constrained by sparse field observations, seasonal surface change, cloud cover, and uncertain event labels. Satellite time series can support repeatable measurement, but anomaly scores alone do not establish a lake-outburst warning system. A credible analysis must bind each pixel window to its source product and acquisition time, separate model fitting from temporal validation and evaluation, represent missing channels explicitly, and report failures as well as supported outcomes. These requirements are especially important when apparent changes may reflect weather, illumination, product processing, or incomplete observations rather than lake change.
The research question is whether a compact, location-aware temporal masked autoencoder yields useful descriptive anomalies for the two specified lake regions under a leakage-controlled multi-year protocol. E1 concerns event timing conditional on an independently reviewed event bracket and operational availability; E2 concerns false alarms over independently verified event-free lake-years; E3 concerns seasonally matched out-of-sample ranks under rolling-origin fitting; E4 is a within-scene zone contrast; and E5 is incremental value against fair, availability-matched baselines. The contributions are a receipt-bound multi-year optical corpus, a missingness-aware patch pipeline, a causal temporal model, and a result packet that leaves unsupported estimands null.
\section*{2. Related work}
Self-supervised Earth-observation models use satellite-specific temporal and spectral structure: SatMAE applies masked pretraining to temporal or multispectral imagery, Presto learns from remote-sensing pixel time series, and Prithvi-EO is an HLS-pretrained geospatial model (Cong et al., 2022; Tseng et al., 2023; Szwarcman et al., 2024). These works establish relevant model families, but do not validate this study's two-site optical anomaly scores or event estimands.
Classical satellite time-series change methods provide comparison families: BFAST models trend and seasonal structure, CCDC processes dense Landsat observations for continuous change detection, and LandTrendr segments spectral trajectories (Verbesselt et al., 2010; Zhu and Woodcock, 2014; Kennedy et al., 2010). The present packet does not claim a fair calibrated comparison against these methods.
Anomaly evaluation depends on protocol as well as score: Wu and Keogh analyze benchmark construction problems, Tatbul et al. formalize range-aware precision and recall, and Sarfraz et al. discuss methodological challenges in unsupervised time-series anomaly detection (Wu and Keogh, 2021; Tatbul et al., 2018; Sarfraz et al., 2024). Event labels, exposure, and alarm operating points remain insufficient for E1, E2, and E5.
Optical and SAR observations have been used retrospectively to examine South Lhonak's 2023 outburst (Yu et al., 2024), alongside regional GLOF risk assessment (Taylor et al., 2023; NRSC/ISRO, 2025). Kumar et al. (2020) describe an ultrasonic early-warning architecture deployed at two other Sikkim lakes. Kahn et al. (2026) is a recent unreviewed preprint that separates site susceptibility from trigger timing and tests simple baselines; it is a novelty challenge, not an independently reproduced benchmark here. This study is optical-only, retrospective, and does not estimate susceptibility, trigger timing, or warning performance.
\section*{3. Data and methods}
The manifest contains 141 observational rows, including source-bound 2023 records. For 2018--2022, score-blind selection chose the lowest-cloud item in each equal-duration bin among full-window products. New COG windows use HTTP 206 range reads, and retained receipts bind source bytes, native baseline, BOA scale/offset, grid, and timestamps. The cumulative 10 GB cap includes earlier attempts and conservative interruption reserves.
Each region uses a nearest-pixel-centered 160 $\times$ 160 EPSG:32645 window at 20 m resolution, summarized to 100 16 $\times$ 16 patches. Clear support is SCL 4/5/6 with at least 25\% valid pixels. Float64 patch-mean B03/B08/B11 are unclamped; absolute index denominators below 0.002 are missing with an explicit status. Candidate zones are FIT-only spectral masks, not validated physical shorelines.
FIT comprises 2018--2021 and Jan--Jun 2023; VAL comprises 2022 and Jul--Sep 2023; the uncertain event bracket is excluded; EVAL begins 2023-10-04 18:30 UTC. All fitted preprocessing, PCA, location-matched references, Score-C moments, and optimizer input are FIT-only. The d=64 factorized model has 207,685 parameters, causal temporal attention, AdamW, cosine decay, EMA, and minimum supported VAL masked-MSE checkpointing.
Score A is masked mean squared error on FIT-standardized patch features, with raw A and ln(A) retained when positive. Score B is same-patch latent distance to FIT observations within $\pm$45 calendar days. Score C is the equal-weight combination ($\alpha$ = 0.5) of FIT-only robust-standardized ln(A) and B; these scores do not declare alarms.
\begin{equation}C_t=\tfrac{1}{2}z_{\mathrm{FIT}}(\ln A_t)+\tfrac{1}{2}z_{\mathrm{FIT}}(B_t)\tag{5}\end{equation}
\noindent The equal weight is $\alpha=0.5$; the coefficient is stated in prose and does not compete with the equation number.

\section*{6. Limitations and conclusion}
This is an exploratory retrospective study of two lake regions; earlier inspection of 2023 outcomes precludes confirmatory interpretation. Corrected training and validation results show that checkpoint selection can use the declared target-bearing VAL blocks, but do not establish outburst detection. Event timing, false-alarm exposure, seasonal generalization and incremental value over baselines remain unestimated because independent event/control labels, adequate rolling-origin years and a calibrated common operating point are unavailable. Candidate zones lack independent geometry validation. Corrected-model EVAL scoring has not been performed. Further work requires event/control review, validated lake geometry, fair baseline calibration and independent scientific and statistical assessment.
