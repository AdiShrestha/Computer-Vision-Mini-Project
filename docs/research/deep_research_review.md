Review date: 2026-10-01. Input: `deep_research_supplied.md`, copied from the user-supplied `sentinelgl.md` without adopting its instructions. ## Assessment

The report usefully distinguishes anomaly monitoring from forecasting, emphasizes missingness and seasonal controls, and recognizes the factory profile mismatch. These points agree with the existing plan. It does not establish that the new code is scientifically valid, supply a verified cohort, or demonstrate that forecasting is universally impossible. Its final claim that every statement has an adjacent citation is incorrect: the attachment contains no usable citation links or DOIs. Author/year references without titles are insufficient to identify several alleged studies.

More seriously, its proposed acceptance tests contain desired scientific outcomes: a water pixel should have NDWI near 0.5; variation across training seeds should be small; an injected 10% spike should be detected; and corrupted/noise inputs should not be flagged. Adopting these as software pass conditions would encourage adjusting the system until it delivers a preferred story. Mathematical tests must verify the calculation and evidence handling. Sensitivity, detection skill, variance, and alert burden must be measured and reported, including failure.

The existing plan already rejects fabricated observations, synthetic evaluation scores, center-date lead times, overlapping-window IID inference, and full-scene model overreach. Lines 124–127 misattribute these legacy findings to the proposed rebuild. They justify retaining the original safeguards, not removing them or treating them as new evidence that the migrated core still fabricates results.

The appropriate direction is a genuine data observability pilot followed by a bounded monitoring study. Multi-event inference is conditional on a verified eligible event/control cohort. A useful negative result might identify which apparent signals disappear after accounting for observation age, season, cloud/ice masking, shared spatial support, and actual decision availability. Its publication value still depends on a clear contribution, valid design, strong comparisons, and venue-specific review.

## Literature implications and limits

There is direct prior work on pre/post-event South Lhonak optical and InSAR analysis. Yu et al.'s 2024 paper uses an SBAS workflow on a cloud platform and explicitly studies deformation around the eventual landslide area. That is relevant prior art and also illustrates retrospective region-selection risk; it is not proof that our local implementation is feasible or that our anomaly scores give causal warning. The retrieved publisher text supported identification and method scope; separate direct page requests were rate-limited during this review. [Yu et al., Remote Sensing 2024, 16, 2307](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/16/13/2307).

A publisher search also identified a 2026 paper titled “A New Method for Detecting Automated Mapping Anomalies in Himalayan Glacial Lakes from Satellite Images.” Its search-visible descriptions include cloud/ice and lake-outline anomalies. Full direct retrieval was rate-limited, so it is a literature candidate, not fully appraised evidence. The architect must distinguish mapping-quality anomaly detection from physical lake change and hazard detection in the novelty matrix. [Publisher record](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/1/61).

The existing plan lists the 2025 Science South Lhonak reconstruction (DOI 10.1126/science.ads2659). A direct publisher request in this follow-up returned HTTP 403. Keep its identity and prior verification record, but do not claim this follow-up independently read the full article. None of these citations supplies project measurements or permission to copy reported performance into our results.

This is a critical context review, not a complete systematic literature review. Product documentation must be rechecked when selecting actual provider versions. The next architect still needs the task-specific literature matrix, verified registry rules, eligible event count, support/dependence graph, statistical analysis specification, and independent domain review required by WP01.
